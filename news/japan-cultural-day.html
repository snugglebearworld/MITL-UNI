Japan Cultural Day showcases student work across all five JLPT levels.

Date   05 October 2025 Venue   Main Hall, MITL Campus Author   MITL Language School Read   6 min

The MITL Language School marked Japan Cultural Day on the first Sunday of October with a full-day programme of student presentations, calligraphy demonstrations, a formal tea ceremony, and an opening reception attended by representatives of the Embassy of Japan, the Japan Foundation Sri Lanka, and JETRO Colombo. Students from all five JLPT levels — from N5 beginners to a small group of N1 candidates — took part in the day's events.

The morning opened with the national anthems of Sri Lanka and Japan, followed by the traditional lighting of the oil lamp by the guest delegation and members of MITL's senior leadership. The hall, decorated with cherry-blossom motifs and the cohort's happi coats, was full from the first session.

Student showcases across five levels

Throughout the day, students presented work across all five JLPT levels in their own classrooms and in the main hall. N5 students introduced themselves and their hometowns in Japanese; N4 cohorts performed short conversational skits drawn from everyday Japanese settings; N3 students delivered a series of three-minute talks on Japanese culture, food and travel; and N2 students worked through a moderated panel discussion in Japanese on the theme of work culture.

Two N1 candidates closed the showcase with a fifteen-minute talk on the differences between formal and informal registers in modern business Japanese — delivered, entirely in Japanese, to an audience that included native Japanese speakers from the visiting delegation.

What stood out today was not the polish of the senior presentations — that is expected at N1 — but the courage and clarity of the N5 students introducing themselves in a language they had only been studying for ten weeks. That is exactly what a language school should make possible. — Mr. Hiroki Oi, Resident Representative, JETRO Colombo

Calligraphy, tea and traditional dress

Visiting calligraphers from the Japan Foundation led an open shodō session in the main hall, with each student leaving with a brushwork piece bearing their name in kanji. A traditional tea ceremony was performed by a guest instructor, with seating prioritised for the N2 and N1 cohorts who had studied the cultural context in their preceding modules.

The cohort of male students chose to attend in matching blue happi coats marked "JAPAN" across the back — a popular cultural-festival garment in Japan — lending the morning audience a vivid, unmistakable colour.

An expanding programme

The day closed with a roundtable between MITL's chairman, the embassy delegation and the visiting JETRO and Japan Foundation representatives, focused on expanding the language school's connections to Japanese employers under the Specified Skilled Worker (SSW) visa programme — a route that several MITL JLPT graduates have already taken into Japan over the past two years.

The next Japan Cultural Day is provisionally scheduled for the same Sunday in October 2026, with the language school planning to host a small group of visiting students and faculty from a Japanese partner institution for the first time.
