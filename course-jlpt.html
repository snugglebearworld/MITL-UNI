About the JLPT

The world's most recognized test of Japanese.

The Japanese-Language Proficiency Test (日本語能力試験) is the official measure of Japanese language competence for non-native speakers, jointly administered by the Japan Foundation and Japan Educational Exchanges and Services. There are five levels — N5 is the easiest and N1 the most advanced.

A JLPT certificate at N2 or above is required by most Japanese employers, the Specified Skilled Worker (SSW) visa programme, and Japanese universities. MITL Campus is a designated registration centre for the December and July sittings.

N5Foundation

N5 — Beginner Foundation 入門

3 months · 8 hrs/weekIntake: MonthlyFee: LKR 28,000

The starting point for absolute beginners. You'll master both hiragana and katakana scripts, learn approximately 100 basic kanji, and build a working vocabulary of around 800 words.

By the end of N5 you can introduce yourself, ask and answer simple questions, read children's books and signs, and follow short conversations spoken slowly.

N3 — Bridge / Intermediate 中級

6 months · 10 hrs/weekIntake: Jan · JulFee: LKR 42,000

The critical "bridge" level between everyday Japanese and the more advanced language used in workplaces and the media. Expect ~650 kanji and a 3,700-word working vocabulary.

N2Business

N2 — Business Ready 中上級

9 months · 12 hrs/weekIntake: Jan · JulFee: LKR 64,000

The level required by most Japanese employers, the Specified Skilled Worker (SSW) visa programme, and many Japanese universities. Approximately 1,000 kanji and 6,000 vocabulary items.

N2 graduates can read newspapers and trade journals in their field, follow detailed workplace discussions, and write formal business correspondence. This is the certificate most career paths in Japan require.

N1Mastery

N1 — Professional Mastery 上級

12 months · 14 hrs/weekIntake: Jan onlyFee: LKR 88,000

The highest level of the JLPT. Required for medical professionals, engineers and academics working in Japan. You'll command approximately 2,000 kanji and 10,000+ vocabulary, and read at near-native speed.

At N1 you can read complex articles on abstract topics, follow lectures and broadcast news, and produce formal written work suitable for academic and professional contexts. Holders are eligible for skilled professional visas.

Career outcomes

Where Japanese opens doors.

From the Specified Skilled Worker programme to academia, here's what each JLPT level unlocks.

SSW Visa (Japan)

Specified Skilled Worker programme across 14 industries — minimum N4 / JFT-Basic; N2 preferred.

Caregiving in Japan

Japanese eldercare and nursing roles increasingly demand N3–N2 plus sector training.

Japanese-language Teacher

Teach Japanese at Sri Lankan language schools and institutions — typically N2 or above.

Translator / Interpreter

Document translation and conference interpreting work — N1 with subject specialism.

Tourism & Hospitality

Hotels, tour operators, and resort chains serving Japanese visitors — N3 and above.

IT & BPO (Japan markets)

Support, QA and engineering teams serving Japanese clients — N2 is the typical bar.

University study in Japan

Undergraduate and graduate programmes — N2 (humanities) or N1 (sciences) plus EJU.

Trading & Manufacturing

Sri Lankan firms with Japanese parent companies — coordination and liaison roles.

Embassy & consular work

Local hire roles at Japanese diplomatic and trade missions — N1 strongly preferred.
